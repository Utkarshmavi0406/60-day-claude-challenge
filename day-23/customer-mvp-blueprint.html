02Ideal Customer Profile
// CRITICAL INSIGHT: THE DUAL CUSTOMER PROBLEM
AIcademy has two distinct customers who require completely different messaging. The student is the end-user (they experience the learning). The parent is the buyer (they sign the cheque). You cannot talk to both the same way. If you market to parents using student language ("it's fun!"), parents won't pay. If you market to students using parent language ("career outcomes"), students won't engage. Design two separate funnels from day one.

04Top 10 Customer Pain Points
#1
⏳
The 11-Year Compounding Loss
Students start learning AI at BTech Year 3 (age ~21). That's 11 years of compounding learning lost since Class 5. At 10% YoY skill compounding, this gap is not recoverable in 2 years of upskilling.
This is the founder's personal story. Lead with it in every marketing message.
#2
💸
Income Delay for Middle-Income Families
Students from ₹5-15L HHI families cannot afford to spend ages 21-23 upskilling for free. Early AI = early freelance income = meaningful family contribution at 16-17. This is Utkarsh's story, and millions share it.
The "earn while in school" angle is the hook that will go viral on LinkedIn/Twitter.
#3
📚
CBSE "AI" is Theory With Zero Practical Output
CBSE introduced "Artificial Intelligence" as a subject in Class 9-12. Students can define "deep learning" but have never run a single line of Python. Zero models built, zero APIs called, zero projects completed.
Position AIcademy as "CBSE AI done right" — same subject, actual hands-on projects.
#4
😱
Parent AI Anxiety is Acute and Unsatisfied
71% of Indian parents are "very concerned" about AI replacing their child's career (EY 2024). The anxiety exists. What doesn't exist is a trusted, structured solution aimed at their specific child's age group.
Don't sell "AI education." Sell "AI-proofing your child's career." The language matters enormously.
#5
👩‍🏫
No Qualified AI Teachers Anywhere
India's 9M school teachers are not equipped to teach AI. Parents know it, principals know it, even students know their computer teacher uses a 2010 syllabus. There is no supply of delivery without solving this.
The founder IS the teacher for the MVP. This is a feature, not a bug — it's the most credible messenger.
#6
🎯
WhiteHat Jr Left a Trust Scar
Millions of parents paid ₹30-80K for WhiteHat Jr only to see quality collapse after BYJU's acquisition. The market has a deep distrust of edtech "promises" — especially around kids and coding. Credibility must be established early.
Show curriculum, show instructor credentials, show a free demo class. Trust is rebuilt through transparency.
#7
🏋️
JEE/NEET Fear Blocks Any "Distraction"
The moment a parent hears "Saturday class", 40% immediately think "JEE prep time lost." AI education that is not framed as complementary to board exam success will face automatic rejection from competitive-exam-focused families.
Frame AI as a JEE math accelerator: "AI tools help students solve problems 3x faster — it's the edge, not a distraction."
#8
🗺️
No Portfolio or Proof of Learning for Students
Students who do coding courses have nothing to show for it — no portfolio, no projects, no proof. Parents who spent ₹40K ask "what did my child actually build?" and there's no answer. The output-visibility gap destroys retention and referrals.
Every AIcademy batch must end with a "Demo Day" — students present projects to parents live. This becomes the marketing content.
#9
🌐
Language and Context Barrier in Generic AI Courses
Most high-quality AI content is in English with Western examples. Indian students in Tier 2 cities need curriculum with Indian context — local datasets, Hindi-English mixed explanations, examples relevant to India's economy and problems.
Design curriculum with Indian context: "Build an AI that predicts monsoon patterns in your city" — instantly more engaging than "build a cat classifier."
#10
🔗
No Bridge Between Learning and Earning
Even the best learning programs end at "completion certificate." There is zero infrastructure to connect a 16-year-old who can build AI automations with a startup that needs those automations built. This is the ultimate market gap nobody has solved in India.
Start building the company relationships now, before you have students. The pipeline promise is what justifies a premium price.

11Top 5 MVP Risks
🔴
Founder Can't Scale as Sole Teacher HIGH
Teaching 20 students every Saturday while working a full-time job (Republic Finance) + MS at UT Dallas is sustainable for 1 batch. At Batch 2 you need another instructor or a co-facilitator. Without this, growth stops at 20 students permanently.
During Batch 1, document every session. Build a "Teaching Playbook." Recruit one MS CS student in India as co-facilitator (willing to work for portfolio + equity). This is your Batch 2 capacity plan.
🔴
Low Demo Class Conversion Rate HIGH
If parents attend a free demo but don't convert to paying students, the model needs revisiting. A conversion rate below 25% suggests the demo experience, price, or trust signals are broken — not that the idea is wrong.
Track conversion obsessively. Survey every non-converter: "What would have made you enroll?" The answer directly tells you what to fix. Target: 40%+ demo-to-paid conversion.
🟡
Time Zone & Coordination Challenge (US-Based Founder) MEDIUM
Saturday 10AM IST = Friday 11:30PM CST. Teaching a class at midnight while managing US work + MS degree is a real fatigue risk. By Month 3, this schedule may break.
Set clear boundaries: one 12-week batch at a time, one section only. Use Batch 1 proceeds to hire an India-based co-facilitator for Batch 2. Alternatively, record sessions and run an async-first model.
🟡
Student Dropout Rate (Engagement Decay) MEDIUM
Online weekend classes for 10-year-olds have a natural dropout risk. Week 1 enthusiasm, Week 4 homework conflict, Week 8 loss of novelty. Without engagement mechanics, dropout rate could exceed 30%.
Add project milestones every 3 weeks. Share student wins on WhatsApp group with parents. Set Week 12 Demo Day as the "public commitment" that keeps students enrolled. Completion rate is your quality metric.
🟡
Getting First 20 Students Without a Marketing Budget MEDIUM
Zero brand awareness, no India operational presence, no existing student network. The first 20 students will not come from Google Ads or an Instagram campaign. They require personal outreach and founder storytelling.
The founder's LinkedIn + Twitter story is the only marketing you need for Batch 1. One viral post about Utkarsh's "Credit Risk Analyst who wishes he'd learned AI at 10" will generate more sign-ups than ₹20,000 in Meta ads.

DAY 15Run Free Demo Class #1. Target: 20+ attendees. Open Zoom, share screen, let students build. Take screenshots of student wins during the session. Record everything.
DAY 16–17Send post-demo survey to all attendees: "Would you enroll your child? What's your hesitation? What's missing?" Then call every parent who attended. Ask directly: "Are you in?"
DAY 18–19Decision gate: If 8+ parents say "yes" → collect payment and confirm Batch 1 dates. If 4–7 say yes → run one more demo and address the objections you heard. If under 4 → you have a messaging problem, not a product problem. Reframe and retest.
DAY 20–21Build your full 12-week curriculum. You already have the outline — now write Week 1-4 in detail (slides, exercises, project briefs). Weeks 5-12 can be drafted as Batch 1 progresses.

DAY 22–24Confirm enrollments, collect payments, send calendar invites for all 12 Saturdays. Create the WhatsApp group for Batch 1 (parents + students separate groups). Send a welcome message from Utkarsh personally.
DAY 25–27Register on Startup India portal for DPIIT recognition (free, takes 2 weeks, gives tax benefits). Open a current account for the business (Razorpay or ICICI Startup Banking). Keep finances clean from Day 1.
DAY 28–30Run Batch 1, Week 1. Document everything: student reactions, parent feedback, what worked, what flopped. Post a LinkedIn recap of "Day 1 of AIcademy" with student screenshots (with permission). This is your Batch 2 marketing.
