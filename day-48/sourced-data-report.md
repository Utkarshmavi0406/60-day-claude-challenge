## Derived / AI-Interpreted Data (explicitly flagged in the app)

**"Relevance to Credit Risk/Finance Roles" score (0-100 per certification)** is the one non-directly-sourced figure in this comparison. It was built by interpreting the qualitative statements above (e.g., Azure's explicit finance/enterprise callout vs. GCP's higher experience bar and smaller enterprise footprint) into a comparable 0-100 scale. This is flagged as **AI-INTERPRETED** throughout the app, distinct from the **SOURCED** tag used on cost, market share, prep time, and validity — all of which trace to a specific citation above.

## Conflicts Identified and How They Were Resolved

1. **AWS prep time had the widest real-world range of the three** — from 2 weeks to 5-6 months depending entirely on the candidate's starting experience. Rather than picking either extreme, the comparison uses a structured 5-week/~50-hour study plan (matching the format of the Azure and Azure sources' "structured plan" framing) as the like-for-like figure, with both the low and high variance disclosed separately in the app's "How this was researched" panel.
2. **No clean, sourced "job postings requiring this certification" count exists for any of the three providers.** Overall cloud infrastructure market share was used as the closest available, genuinely sourced proxy for job market demand, and this substitution is disclosed explicitly rather than presented as a direct jobs count.
3. **Validity periods differ far more than expected** (1 year for Azure vs. 2 for GCP vs. 3 for AWS) — this wasn't one of the four weighted criteria the interview asked for, so it's shown as supplementary context in the comparison table rather than folded into the weighted score, but it's surfaced prominently since it materially affects long-term cost of ownership.

---

## Most Surprising Finding

The AWS Certified Machine Learning Engineer – Associate — currently the top-ranked option under equal weighting — is being retired within months of this research. Anyone planning to sit MLA-C01 needs to confirm the exam version at registration, since MLA-C02 opens September 1, 2026, right as MLA-C01 stops being offered in English on September 28, 2026. This wasn't something I was looking for; it surfaced directly from AWS's own official certification page during routine research and materially affects the timing of a real decision.
